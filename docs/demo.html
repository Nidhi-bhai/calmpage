Why Reading on the Web Got So Hard

By A. Writer · Demo page

You opened a link to read one article. Before the first sentence, the page asked you to accept cookies, subscribe to a newsletter, and download an app. A red header followed you down the page, and a banner ad flashed between paragraphs.

None of that is the article. The article is a few hundred words of text and maybe one picture. Everything else is the site asking for something: your consent, your email, your attention, or a tap on an ad.

On Hacker News, people keep asking for the same fix. One wrote that they would pay money for a browser plugin that restyles every web page to a single readable design they like. Another asked for an extension that toggles the whole browsing session into reader mode. A third said they would pay for a way to kill obstructive pop-ups, and named a price.

Browsers already have a reader view, but you have to click it on every page, it can't be set for a whole session, and the pop-ups usually still block the page before you get there. Cookie banners in particular often sit on top of the reader button itself.

What a calm page looks like

A calm page has one column, a comfortable line length of 60 to 75 characters, a font size you can read without leaning in, and enough space between lines. It has no fixed bars, no modals and no scroll lock. It looks the same on every site, because it is your design, not theirs.
